# Guide skeleton

Voice: instructional, warm, direct. Address the reader as "you". Register: straight by default, light playful framing acceptable if the user asks. A guide teaches a reader to do or understand something well, going deeper than a tutorial and broader than a runbook.

Candidate shape:

- **Title and intro** — what the guide covers and who it is for, in a short paragraph. Set the scope and the assumed starting point.
- **Background** — the concepts a reader needs before the how. Explain the why, and use an analogy where a concept is genuinely hard.
- **The core sections** — grouped logically by task or concept, in the order a reader would work through them. Each section mixes a lead-in, the working detail (commands, tables, diagrams), and an interpretive close.
- **Common pitfalls** — the mistakes people actually make, and how to avoid them.
- **Next steps** — where to go deeper, with inline links.

Prefer descriptive headings over rhetorical questions. Cite tools, services, and limits inline where a reader would verify them.
