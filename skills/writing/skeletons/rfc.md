# RFC skeleton

Voice: neutral, declarative, precise. Register: straight. An RFC proposes a change and invites structured feedback before a decision. It should be complete enough to argue with and clear enough to reach consensus on.

Frontmatter extras for this type: `status` (Draft / In review / Accepted / Rejected), `authors`, `reviewers`, `discussion` link.

Candidate shape:

- **Summary** — the proposal in a short paragraph a reviewer can grasp without reading further.
- **Motivation** — the problem and why it is worth solving now. Be concrete about the cost of doing nothing.
- **Proposal** — the change in detail, with diagrams and interfaces where they clarify. State the recommendation, then the alternatives considered and why each lost.
- **Alternatives considered** — the serious options, each with its trade-off, so reviewers see the design space.
- **Drawbacks and risks** — what this costs and what threatens it, honestly.
- **Open questions** — what is still undecided and where you want reviewer input.

An RFC is one type where a trailing References section is acceptable when it cites many sources. Otherwise link inline. Keep the tone even and the claims checkable.
